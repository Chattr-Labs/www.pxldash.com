128 × 64 RGB LED · on your wall

Your wall, lit by what's happening now.

PxlDash drives a small LED panel that hangs like a picture and shows one thing at a time: the flight you're waiting on, the match you're following, or the weather when nothing else is live.

See the boards How it works

One hero per panel

One thing at a time, readable from across the room.

Every board has a single hero — a score, a route, the time — with a little support underneath. With several modules on, the wall becomes a queue, not a grid. Whatever is happening right now goes first.

  1. TAKEOVER A goal, a touchdown, a gate change. It owns the wall, then hands it back.
  2. LIVE In progress, with a clock. Short dwell, fast refresh.
  3. UPCOMING Countdowns and next-ups, on a slower cadence.
  4. AMBIENT The resting state — the time and the weather when nothing else qualifies.

Flights

Your trip, from schedule to gate.

Import a schedule from its calendar feed, or paste it in. The panel walks each leg through its day — pending, pre-flight, taxiing, in the air, landed, layover — with live position and the weather at each end. Between trips it tells you what's flying over the house.

Sports

Your teams lead the wall.

Live scores with the game clock, field position, and flags or team marks. When someone scores, the panel plays the celebration on its own, then goes back to the game. College football is on the wall today, alongside a World Cup board for the whole house.

How it works

The server decides. The panel draws.

  1. 01

    Data comes in

    Flight status, scores and weather are fetched on the server, on a cadence that speeds up when something's live — about every 20 seconds during a match, slower when nothing's on.

  2. 02

    A scene goes out

    Each board is laid out as a handful of widgets — text, a score, a progress rail, a clock — and sent to the panel as finished pixels or as a scene it draws and animates itself.

  3. 03

    Nothing to reflash

    New boards and design changes ship from the website. When the firmware does need an update, the panel fetches it over the air.
